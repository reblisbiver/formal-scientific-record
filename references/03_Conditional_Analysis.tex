\section{Grouping and estimand}
For trials with an observed previous choice, let $y_t$ be the realized absolute choice and $\widehat y_t$ its prediction. The analysis unit is a trial, not a participant:
\begin{equation}
G_t=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Stay},&y_t=y_{t-1},\\\mathrm{Switch},&y_t\ne y_{t-1},\end{cases}\qquad
\widehat p_g=\frac{c_g}{n_g},\quad c_g=\sum_{t:G_t=g}\mathbf1\{\widehat y_t=y_t\},\quad n_g=\sum_t\mathbf1\{G_t=g\}.
\end{equation}
The first trial of each sequence is excluded because its preceding choice is unavailable. For each group, $n_g$ is the number of eligible trials and $c_g$ the number of correct predictions.

\section{Interval definition and scope}
For group count $(c,n)$, $\widehat p=c/n$ and $z=1.96$, the displayed interval is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Wilson}_{.95}(c,n)=\frac{\widehat p+z^2/(2n)\ \pm\ z\sqrt{\widehat p(1-\widehat p)/n+z^2/(4n^2)}}{1+z^2/n}.
\end{equation}
This interval assumes independent Bernoulli trials. It does \emph{not} account for within-participant dependence; participant identifiers, participant-level uncertainty and hypothesis-test results are absent. It must not be interpreted as participant-level inference.
