\section{Question and controlled design}
With the same input, recurrent architecture, training protocol, test examples and evaluation space, compare two target encodings. The only designated change is the learning target.
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{.19\linewidth}LL@{}}
\toprule
 & Baseline $B$ & Treatment $T$\\
\midrule
Input & RAW3 & RAW3\\
Architecture & GRU & GRU\\
Training target & $y_t\in\{0,1\}$ & $z_t=\mathbf1\{y_t\ne y_{t-1}\}$\\
Evaluation target & $y_t$ & $y_t$ (inverse-mapped)\\
Protocol & \multicolumn{2}{l}{Paired seeds ($n=8$); same reported splits and metrics}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
For $t\ge2$, the treatment prediction is mapped to the same absolute-choice outcome as the baseline:
\begin{equation}
q_t=P_T(z_t=1),\qquad
P_T(y_t=1)=\begin{cases}q_t,&y_{t-1}=0,\\1-q_t,&y_{t-1}=1.\end{cases}\label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
The mapping is defined only for $t\ge2$. The synthetic fixture does not specify a first-trial convention or establish the exact aggregation range; it also contains no independently documented study preregistration.
